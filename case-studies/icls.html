Consumer Research Platform
Seven tools. One front door.

ICLS unified a fragmented consumer-research estate behind a single entry point — an answer engine over historical studies, five capability groups organised by intent, and a governed request-to-approval workflow with spend visible in one view.

Tools used
    01 — Problem

    Seven specialist tools, and no front door

    Consumer research ran across seven disconnected specialist tools. Researchers memorised where each one lived, approvals were tracked in email threads, and completed studies were archived somewhere unsearchable — so the same research was commissioned twice.

    • 01Fragmented estate

      Seven tools with separate entry points, separate logins and no shared vocabulary between them.

    • 02Approvals lived in email

      Status existed only in someone's inbox. "Where is my study?" had no authoritative answer.

    • 03Past research was invisible

      Completed studies were archived but not searchable, so prior work was rarely reused.

    This looked like a UI brief and was really an information architecture and governance brief. The decisions that mattered were structural, not visual.

    02 — Research

    Five roles, one contested workflow

    UserPrimary goal
    Study Owner / ResearcherDesign and execute a study without relearning the tool estate
    Approving ManagerReview and sign off study design on a defensible basis
    Finance ApproverValidate budget before commitment
    Portfolio AdminOversee the portfolio and report fiscal-year spend
    Lead SupplierReceive scope and deliver against agreed terms
    Key findings
    • 01Onboarding took weeks

      New researchers learned tool locations by asking colleagues, not by using the product.

    • 02"Where is my study?"

      The single most-asked question in the estate — and the one nothing could answer.

    • 0320+ governed fields

      The PO request carried more than twenty fields, each with its own compliance rule.

    • 04Search intent was conversational

      People asked questions in sentences, not keywords, and expected an answer.

    • 05Compliance arrived too late

      AI, IT and legal declarations surfaced after commitment, forcing rework.

    • 06Finance needed one number

      Portfolio owners wanted a single fiscal-year spend figure, self-serve.

    03 — Insight

    Organise by intent, not by inventory

    • Intent beats inventory

      Users do not think "which tool" — they think "I need to learn something". Grouping by intent made seven tools legible as five capabilities.

    • Search is the homepage

      If the top question is conversational, the answer engine belongs above the fold, framed as a question rather than a search box.

    • Status is an object

      Approval stage deserved to be a first-class entity with a named owner and a timestamp — not a line in an email.

    • Governance belongs in flow

      Compliance declarations captured inside the request, with inline guidance, prevent rework after commitment.

    06 — Core Flows

    Discover, request, approve

    Three flows carry the platform. The first prevents duplicate research, the second governs commissioning, the third makes status self-evident.

    Discovery flow
    1. 01Ask

      Natural-language question, not keywords.

    2. 02Retrieve

      Matching historical studies surface.

    3. 03Evaluate

      Scope, method, markets, deliverables.

    4. 04Decide

      Reuse existing or commission new.

    5. 05Route

      Hand off to the right capability.

    Study request flow
    1. 01Define

      Title, owner, organisation, function.

    2. 02Scope

      Category, technique, methodology, markets.

    3. 03Declare

      Supplier, IT, AI and legal compliance.

    4. 04Justify

      Purpose, success criteria, background.

    5. 05Submit

      Save as draft or commit the study.

    Approval & execution flow
    1. 01Planned

      Study created and scoped.

    2. 02Design Approval

      Manager signs off on method.

    3. 03Financial Approval

      Finance validates the budget.

    4. 04Execution

      Supplier delivers against scope.

    5. 05Completed

      Deliverables filed and searchable.

    Every transition records step, date, status and owner — so the audit trail builds itself rather than being reconstructed later.

    08 — Design Principles

    Four rules that settled every argument

    • 01Intent before inventory

      Group by what a person is trying to do, never by which team owns the tool.

    • 02Status is always visible

      Every study shows its stage and its current owner without anyone having to ask.

    • 03Governance in the flow

      Compliance is captured where the work happens, not bolted on after commitment.

    • 04Reuse before commission

      Show what already exists before offering to spend money on something new.

    10 — Readability

    A 20-field form that people finish

    The governed request was the densest surface in the product. Legibility was solved at three levels.

    • FORMSectioned disclosure

      General Study Information and Key Information split a 20-field request into two readable passes.

    • PORTFOLIOStatus as colour

      Nine colour-coded work statuses make the exceptions in a long table visible before it is read.

    • PROGRESSFractions, not flags

      PO upload renders as a progress fraction, so partial completion is legible at a glance.

    Supporting patterns
    PatternWhy it exists
    Governed pickersCategory, technique, methodology and markets come from centrally maintained lists, so data stays comparable across the portfolio
    Inline guidanceEach declaration carries its rule beside the field, removing the need to consult a separate policy document
    Dual currencyAmounts render in local currency and USD together, so global markets and finance read the same record
    11 — Testing & Iteration

    What changed once researchers used it

    Problem

    The long request form was abandoned midway when a researcher had to go and find a supplier detail.

    →
    Solution

    Save as Draft, with drafts surfaced in My Studies so an unfinished request is never lost.

    Problem

    A keyword search box returned nothing useful, because people typed whole questions.

    →
    Solution

    An answer engine framed as a question, with popular-search prompts to teach the interaction.

    Problem

    Compliance declarations were misread, causing requests to be returned after financial commitment.

    →
    Solution

    Guidance authored inline beside every AI, IT and legal declaration at the point of answering.

    Problem

    Approvers could not tell whether a delay was theirs or somebody else's.

    →
    Solution

    Approval history recording step, date, status and assignee for every transition.

    12 — Design System

    Built on the client design system

    ICLS was composed from the client's existing enterprise component library, extended only where the governance workflow demanded new patterns.

    Components extended
    • Approval stepper

      A five-stage horizontal stepper carrying stage name, owner and timestamp — reused on every study record.

    • Governed field group

      A picker paired with inline guidance, used for every centrally maintained taxonomy field.

    • Portfolio table

      Column filters, nine status colours, progress fractions, refresh and Excel export in one dense surface.

    The value was consistency of meaning — a status colour means the same thing on the home hub, the portfolio table and the study record.

    15 — Reflection

    Architecture was the design

    This project looked like a UI brief and was really an information architecture and governance brief. The decisions that mattered most were structural: naming groups by intent, treating status as an object, and putting compliance inside the flow. None of them are visible as a component, and all of them are why the platform works.

    • What I learned

      Unifying an estate is a diplomacy problem before it is a design problem. The IA tree was the artefact that got seven tool owners to agree, and it unblocked the build more than any screen did.

    • What I would improve today

      A personalised home based on role and study history, predictive approval-time estimates, AI-assisted form completion from prior studies, and a dedicated WCAG 2.1 AA pass on the dense portfolio table.
