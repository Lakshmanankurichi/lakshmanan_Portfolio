Tools used
    01 — Problem

    Deadlines are fixed. Penalties are real.

    Every payment goes to a specific county tax agency, has a hard due date and can be submitted by web, email or an automated channel. A missed or wrong payment is a financial and compliance liability.

    • 01Dense queues

      Hundreds of contracts across agencies, with no clear sense of what needed attention first.

    • 02Hidden risk

      High-liability items looked the same as routine ones until something went wrong.

    • 03Scattered status

      Open, in-review and action-required items had to be pieced together from different views.

    The design problem underneath: help operators answer “what must I act on today, and why?” in seconds.

    02 — My Role

    What I owned

    • 01Workflow mapping

      Mapped the end-to-end lifecycle from obligation to confirmed payment.

    • 02Status & risk model

      Defined one status and risk vocabulary used across every view.

    • 03Validation & exceptions

      Wrote validation and exception-handling rules with BAs and engineering.

    • 04AI-assisted delivery

      Prototyped and specified flows with AI tooling to shorten review cycles.

    03 — Users

    Framed around the job, not the data

    Operators work against the calendar

    Their day is a queue sorted by due date and risk. Leaders need the portfolio view: spend, savings, cycle time and exposure.

    • Operator: clear the queue accurately before due dates
    • Specialist reviewer: sign off high-liability items
    • Leader: monitor spend, trends and items at risk
    UserSurfacePrimary goal
    OperatorWebClear the queue accurately before due dates
    Specialist reviewerWebReview and approve high-liability items
    LeaderWeb + MobileSee spend, savings, cycle time and risk at a glance
    04 — Constraints

    Realities that shaped the design

    ConstraintDesign response
    Hard due dates per agencyDue date as a sortable primary column, in one date format (YYYY-MM-DD)
    High-liability items need specialist reviewA system-level warning banner with a direct “Action required” entry point
    Multiple submission channelsA method column: W · Web, E · Email, A · Automated
    Very large tablesSortable headers, queue search and “View details” on every row
    Leaders on the moveKPIs, warning and trend chart reflow into a single mobile column

    06 — Status & Risk

    One vocabulary for every screen

    Before any screen design, I defined what each state means. The same labels appear in the queue, the details view and on mobile, always as text plus colour, never colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1).

    07 — Final Design

    A workspace built around attention

    Dashboard anatomy
    • 01Warning first

      High-liability tasks surface as a banner above the KPIs, with one “Action required” entry point into the filtered queue.

    • 02KPI cards

      Total spend, realised savings, average cycle time and at-risk POs, each with a trend and change since last period.

    • 03Active queue

      Contract, supplier, agency, amount, due date, status, risk and method, with “View details” on every row.

    Values in the mock-ups are illustrative sample data, not client figures.

    08 — Accessibility

    Built in, not bolted on

    • Colour + text

      Status and risk always carry a text label (WCAG 1.4.1).

    • Readable data

      Table text at AA contrast; amounts right-aligned for comparison.

    • Keyboard-first tables

      Sortable headers and row actions are reachable and announced.

    • Charts with labels

      Legends, values and peak call-outs in text, not colour alone.

    09 — Impact

    What it added up to

    • Risk surfaces first

      High-liability items appear above the work instead of hiding in the list.

    • One shared language

      The same status and risk vocabulary across views and roles.

    • Faster queue work

      Sortable, scannable rows with one clear next action.

    • Leadership visibility

      Spend, savings, cycle time and exposure on desktop and mobile.

    10 — Reflection

    In compliance products, clarity is control

    The most valuable decision wasn't visual. It was agreeing what “Action required” and “High risk” mean, and then showing those states the same way everywhere.

    • What I learned

      Warnings must sit above the work. When risk lives inside a details page, nobody sees it in time.

    • What I would add next

      Due-date forecasting, bulk actions for routine items, and AI-assisted anomaly flags on amounts and agencies.
