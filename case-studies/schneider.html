    01 — Problem

    Many products. Two kinds of buyer.

    The catalogue spanned switches and plates, MCBs, smart cameras, lighting and cables, each with variants, finishes and specifications. Product data came from different sources and wasn't always complete.

    • 01Technical catalogue

      Many variants made finding the right product slow.

    • 02Two buyer types

      Retail wanted to browse and buy. Trade buyers needed bulk orders and quotes.

    • 03Uneven data

      Components had to survive missing images, prices or attributes.

    02 — My Role

    What I owned

    • 01Information architecture

      Navigation and entry points for both buyer types.

    • 02Component system

      Product card, category tile and listing patterns.

    • 03Data-aware design

      Field and fallback rules agreed with developers.

    • 04Accessibility

      Accessible patterns built into components from the start.

    03 — Users

    Trade searches. Retail browses.

    Two journeys, one storefront

    Electricians and distributors usually know the exact product and want speed. Homeowners compare and need confidence.

    • Trade: search by name or code, add many items, request a quote
    • Retail: browse categories, compare price and rating, check out
    StepRetail buyerTrade buyer
    ArriveHero campaign or categoryGlobal search
    EvaluatePrice, offer, ratingSpecification, availability
    CommitAdd to cart → checkoutBulk Order / RFQ → quote
    04 — Constraints

    Realities that shaped the design

    ConstraintDesign response

    Quote-led trade purchasingSeparate Bulk Order / RFQ entry point
    Spec-heavy comparisonKey specs surfaced on listing; full specs on detail
    Brand consistencyBrand blue header, neutral product backgrounds

    07 — Component Spec

    Product card anatomy and data rules

    This is the spec that let one component scale across the whole catalogue.

    ElementRuleIf data is missing
    Product imageRequired · 1:1 · neutral backgroundCategory placeholder
    NameRequired · max 2 linesCard not published
    RatingOptionalHidden, with no empty stars
    Price + original priceBoth shown when discounted“Request price” → RFQ
    Offer badgeOnly when an offer is activeHidden
    Add to cartLabelled icon button · ≥ 44px targetReplaced by “Request quote”
    Before / after
    Before

    Cards broke on missing data: empty stars, blank prices and stretched images.

    →
    After

    Every field has a rule and a fallback, so the grid stays consistent at any level of data completeness.

    08 — Accessibility

    Built into the components

    • Named icon buttons

      Search, account, wishlist and cart have accessible names.

    • Offers in text

      Discounts shown as price text, not colour alone.

    • Keyboard carousels

      Previous/next controls reachable and operable.

    • Target sizes

      Add-to-cart and controls at 44px minimum.

    09 — Impact

    What it added up to

    • One storefront

      Retail, trade and distributor buyers served from a single experience.

    • Scalable components

      Data-aware cards and tiles that hold up as the catalogue grows.

    • Quote-to-cart

      Dedicated Bulk Order / RFQ path for quote-led buying.

    • Consistency

      Accessible, uniform product presentation.

    10 — Reflection

    In catalogue e-commerce, the data shapes the design

    Designing for missing data, not ideal data, is what made the system hold up.

    • What I learned

      Agree field rules with engineering before polishing visuals. The edge cases are where the design gets tested.

    • What I would add next

      Spec-based filtering and comparison, saved trade lists for repeat orders, and reorder from order history.
